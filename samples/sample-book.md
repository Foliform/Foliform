### A short sample manuscript

By Alex Rowan

# Chapter One: A Room for Ideas

## The first blank page

The room was quiet except for the rain tapping at the window. Mara opened
her notebook and waited for a sentence to arrive. Nothing did. The blank
page seemed less like an invitation and more like a question:
**What is worth saying?**

She wrote the date in the corner. Then she wrote one ordinary thing she had
noticed on the walk home: a paper boat turning slowly in the gutter. It was
not a story yet, but it was something real enough to begin with.

> “You do not have to see the whole staircase. Just take the first step.”
> — Martin Luther King Jr.

Mara underlined *first step*. The words looked simple on the page. Simple
was useful. A writer could carry a simple thing forward and see what it
became.

::: pullquote
A beginning does not need to be grand. It only needs to be honest.
— From Mara's notebook
:::

## Gathering small details

She made a list of things the rain had changed:

- The streetlights had halos.
- The pavement held a second, upside-down city.
- The paper boat had found the fastest current.
- Someone had left a yellow umbrella by the gate.

The details began to gather around one another. A child might have folded
the boat. Someone might be looking for the umbrella. The street might lead
somewhere Mara had never been.

----

She turned the page and wrote a question at the top:

**Who is the boat trying to find?**

The question made her smile. It was small enough to answer, and answering
it might lead to another question. For tonight, that was plenty.

# Chapter Two: Finding the Shape

## A map, not a cage

The next morning, Mara drew a map of the story. It was a loose map, full of
arrows and crossed-out ideas. She marked the moments she wanted to reach,
but left the path between them open.

| Moment | What changes | Detail to remember |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The boat appears | Curiosity begins | Rainwater in the gutter |
| A note is found | The mystery deepens | Blue ink on white paper |
| The owner returns | The story turns | A yellow umbrella |
| The boat is released | The story resolves | A stream after the rain |

A plan can help a writer keep their bearings. It should not prevent a
surprising turn. When a better idea appears, the map can change.

## Making room for revision

Mara's first draft moved too quickly. She had written what happened, but
not what it felt like to wait in the rain or discover a note folded inside
a paper boat.

She returned to the opening and slowed it down. She gave the street a
sound, the water a color, and her character a reason to hesitate. The scene
did not need more events. It needed more attention.

```text
First draft: The boat moved down the street.

Revision: The little boat spun once in the gutter,
then slipped beneath the bridge of a fallen leaf.
```

## Keeping the useful parts

Revision is not the same as making every sentence longer. It is a way of
finding what belongs. Mara read each paragraph aloud and listened for the
places where the rhythm stumbled.

She kept the paper boat. She removed a whole page about the weather. She
changed the ending twice, then returned to the first version with a new
understanding of why it worked.

# Chapter Three: The Last Page

## A place to pause

By the end of the week, Mara had a complete draft. It was not perfect, but
it had a beginning, a middle, and an ending that felt earned. She saved a
copy, made a cup of tea, and read the story from the first line to the last.

This time she was not looking for mistakes alone. She was looking for the
experience of reading: where the story invited her forward, where it asked
her to pause, and whether its final image stayed with her.

> A finished draft is not the end of the work.
> It is the first version you can finally see whole.

There were still changes to make. Some words could be clearer; one scene
needed a little more space. But the shape was there, and now she knew what
to improve.

## Begin again

Mara closed the notebook. Outside, the rain had stopped. In the gutter, a
small stream carried leaves toward the end of the street.

She opened to a fresh page and wrote another date.
